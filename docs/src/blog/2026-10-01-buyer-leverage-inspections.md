  <h2>Quick take</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>A gradually rebalancing Greater Lansing market gives many buyers more options than early 2026.</li>
    <li>That leverage is best spent on inspections, credits, and timeline clarity — not theatrical low offers on well-priced homes.</li>
    <li>Waiving protections to “win” a house you have not studied is still an expensive habit.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>For first-time buyers</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Keep inspection rights unless your advisor has a specific, property-level reason not to.</li>
    <li>Pre-decide repair vs credit vs walk criteria so negotiation stays calm after the report.</li>
    <li>If two homes fit, compete hard on the better one and stay disciplined on the stretch.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>For investors</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Treat inspection findings as underwriting inputs, not surprises after you are emotionally closed.</li>
    <li>Capex over the first 12–24 months should be in the offer math before you lock rate and close date.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Working with an advisor</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>A short consult beats reacting to the loudest listing alert. Map band, financing, and must-haves first — then write.</li>
  </ul>
