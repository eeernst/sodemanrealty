Buyer Leverage Is Returning — Use It on Diligence, Not Drama

With more listings to compare, inspection and contingency strategy matter more than waving every protection.

Quick take

  • A gradually rebalancing Greater Lansing market gives many buyers more options than early 2026.
  • That leverage is best spent on inspections, credits, and timeline clarity — not theatrical low offers on well-priced homes.
  • Waiving protections to “win” a house you have not studied is still an expensive habit.

For first-time buyers

  • Keep inspection rights unless your advisor has a specific, property-level reason not to.
  • Pre-decide repair vs credit vs walk criteria so negotiation stays calm after the report.
  • If two homes fit, compete hard on the better one and stay disciplined on the stretch.

For investors

  • Treat inspection findings as underwriting inputs, not surprises after you are emotionally closed.
  • Capex over the first 12–24 months should be in the offer math before you lock rate and close date.

Working with an advisor

  • A short consult beats reacting to the loudest listing alert. Map band, financing, and must-haves first — then write.
